# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Billy Rovzar (CEO, Lemon Studios) and the Lemon story department: producers, development executives, and writers. Not engineers. They work bilingually in Spanish and English, often dictating by voice, usually with several projects open at once. Their context: developing film and TV projects (drama, comedy, horror) and needing to know where a story naturally wants to go before committing writers and budget.

## Product Purpose

MiroFish Lemon rehearses film and TV storylines inside a multi-agent simulation: seed a story world, let dozens of AI character-agents live it out on simulated social platforms, then read a prediction report about the emergent drama. It exists to answer "what does this story do when nobody is steering it?" before a writers' room burns weeks finding out. Success: a producer runs a simulation without help, trusts the cast on screen, and walks away with a report the story department actually reads and quotes in a pitch.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, dark, dramatic. The app should feel like a private screening room where a story's future is being projected: focused light on the material, everything else recedes. Emotional goals: immersion in the story world, confidence in the evidence, a sense of occasion when a simulation run reveals something. Not playful, not corporate, not neon.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-SaaS dashboards: gradient heroes, big-number metric cards, purple-blue gradients, identical icon-heading-text card grids.
- Enterprise software chrome: dense toolbars, tiny gray text, settings-heavy screens in the style of Jira or SAP.
- Cyberpunk everywhere: the terminal/hex-ID aesthetic belongs to the Character Engine sub-app only; it must not leak into the main product.

## Design Principles

1. The story is the interface. Screens exist to make narrative legible (characters, arcs, collisions), never to showcase engine telemetry. Jargon like console output, iterations, and tool counts stays out of the producer's line of sight.
2. Evidence over assertion. Every claim the product makes about a story is traceable to what a character actually said or did in the simulation; quotes are first-class UI material.
3. Producer-first, engineer-never. Anyone in the story department can run the full pipeline without reading a manual; anything that needs a terminal is a defect.
4. Cinematic focus, theatrical restraint. One dramatic focal point per screen, darkness used as staging rather than decoration; drama comes from the story content, not from UI effects.
5. Bilingual by design. Spanish and English are equal citizens in input, interface, and output; language is a project setting, never an accident.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Dark surfaces must hold 4.5:1 text contrast. Full keyboard operability, visible focus states, respected reduced-motion preferences. Voice-first friendly: every long-form input works with macOS dictation. No color-only status signaling (pair color with text or icon).
